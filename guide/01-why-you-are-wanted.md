# 1. Why you are wanted

The Drupal AI Initiative builds the `drupal/ai` module ecosystem: providers, agents, chatbots, CKEditor tools, translation, content review, the Context Control Center (CCC), and the public [AI demo](https://new.drupal.org/ai/demo). Most contributors are developers. The UI they ship is reviewed by a small UX track that is short on hands.

Three lanes, all with design work in them:

- **Inside AI** — AI features inside Drupal's admin UI. Chat panels, review sidebars, settings forms, menu placement. Most UX requests land here.
- **Outside AI** — external agents (Claude Code, Cursor) talking to Drupal through MCP and the Tool API. Little UI; occasional naming and documentation work.
- **People** — how site builders, editors and reviewers see what the AI did and why. The CCC "People" stream is a discovery and information-architecture problem, not a coding one.

What the UX track has done recently: the Rotterdam demo site, story-by-story usability reviews of the demo with Figma decks, low-fidelity explorations of the Ask AI chat controls, and an AI admin menu hierarchy now used across modules.

What you bring that is missing: cold-read usability testing, content design for labels and help text, accessibility review, and sketches that turn a list of problems into a proposal. The track's own rule: every review pairs with low-fidelity UI exploration so reviews produce solutions, not only problems.

The track's official page: [How to Contribute to UX](https://project.pages.drupalcode.org/ai/1.4.x/ux/how_to_contribute/). It asks for UX plus Drupal experience and ongoing involvement. Read it as a preference. Story reviews are being done by people new to Drupal; the demo is built for them.
